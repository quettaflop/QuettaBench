The purpose of this file is to give the cross-validation harness a frozen,
reproducible slice of natural-looking English text. Mixture-of-experts models
route each token to a small set of specialist subnetworks, and the choice of
experts depends on the content flowing through the model. Synthetic token
patterns exercise the router in ways that no real workload ever would, so a
benchmark that feeds only synthetic identifiers can land in a corner of the
routing space that flatters one engine and punishes another. Real prose, even
plain prose about benchmarking itself, produces the kind of mixed routing
that inference servers actually see in production.

A decode benchmark is a simple thing on the surface. Load the weights, prefill
a prompt, then measure how long each additional token takes. Underneath that
surface sit a dozen decisions that quietly shape the number: how the key and
value cache is laid out, whether the kernel launches are captured into a graph
and replayed, how the tensor-parallel ranks exchange partial sums, whether the
scheduler batches requests eagerly or lazily, and how the measurement itself
is taken. Two teams can benchmark the same model on the same hardware and
publish numbers that differ by a factor of two without either of them lying.
The difference lives in the configuration, and the cure is to write every
relevant setting into the log next to the number it produced.

Tensor parallelism splits each weight matrix across several accelerators, so
every layer ends with a reduction that stitches the partial results back
together. On machines with a fast direct interconnect the reduction is cheap
and the split is close to free. On machines connected through a slower bus the
reduction becomes the tax that every layer pays, and past a certain batch size
the tax dominates the arithmetic. This is why a batch of sixty-four sequences
can be communication-bound on one node and compute-bound on another, and why
a fair comparison must either match the interconnect or refuse to compare.

Expert parallelism adds a second layer of geography. The specialists are
scattered across the accelerators, and a token must travel to wherever its
chosen experts live. When every sequence in a batch is identical, every token
chooses the same experts, and the entire batch queues at one or two devices
while the rest sit idle. When the sequences differ, the work spreads out and
the same hardware finishes the step in a fraction of the time. Neither regime
is wrong, but they are different experiments, and a table that mixes them is
comparing apples with oranges while wearing a straight face.

The caching hierarchy matters as much as the compute. A key-value cache stored
in eight-bit floating point moves half the bytes of one stored in sixteen-bit
form, and at long context lengths the cache traffic, not the matrix math, sets
the pace of each step. An engine holding its cache in the wider format pays
for that choice on every token, and the honest way to present the resulting
gap is to label it as a storage-format difference rather than an engine
deficiency. Labels are cheap; unlabeled asymmetries are expensive, because a
reader will attribute the whole gap to code quality.

Measurement method is the last quiet variable. A loop that synchronizes with
the host after every step exposes launch latency that a pipelined loop hides.
A slope fitted across several generation lengths cancels fixed overheads that
a single timed window absorbs. Comparing a synchronized median against a
pipelined slope produces a number that looks precise and means very little.
The remedy is boring and effective: use the same method on both sides, print
the method into the log, and let the table refuse to print a ratio whenever
the methods disagree.

Reproducibility, in the end, is a checklist rather than a virtue. Freeze the
inputs, pin the kernels, record the environment, name the commit, and keep the
raw logs. A number that arrives with its provenance can be defended a year
later by someone who never ran the experiment. A number that arrives alone is
just a rumor with digits, and rumors do not survive review. This paragraph
ends the corpus with a reminder that the text itself is part of the frozen
configuration: change this file and the routing changes with it, so treat its
checksum as part of the benchmark's identity.
